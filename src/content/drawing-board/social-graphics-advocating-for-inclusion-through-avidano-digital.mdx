<TextBlock className="margin-x-auto">

Through my independent practice, Avidano Digital, I’ve been creating social graphics about disability rights and inclusion. They give me room to question the assumptions I see in accessibility work and share views that don’t always fit inside a client project.

</TextBlock>

<TextBlock className="margin-x-auto">

## Access is a right

These graphics challenge a familiar argument: accessibility helps everyone. That can be true, but disabled people should not have to prove a benefit to others before their access matters.

</TextBlock>

<TextBlock className="margin-x-auto">

“Nothing about us without us” carries a related message: disabled people need a say in the decisions that affect them.

The Progress Pride flag in “Inclusion is not one-dimensional” brings disability and other parts of a person’s identity into the same conversation.

</TextBlock>

## Designing the message

I used bold color and large type to make each point clear. The graphics share Avidano Digital’s branding, while the imagery changes with the message.

Pixel-art characters in “Accessibility isn’t a game” question the idea of accessibility as a score to chase.

</TextBlock>

<FigureSingle width="medium" className="margin-x-auto" caption="Pixel-art graphics challenging the idea of a perfect accessibility score.">

  <Image
    src={accessibilityIsntAGame}
    alt="Two colorful Avidano Digital graphics with pixel-art characters and the headline “Accessibility isn’t a game.” Each explains that automated tools can catch issues, but human testing is needed to understand whether a site works for people with disabilities."
  />

</FigureSingle>

<TextBlock className="margin-x-auto">

Writing for this format takes care. I want each statement to be direct without reducing the people or issues behind it to a slogan.

For “These words were flagged for removal,” I arranged words about identity, disability, and equity into an American flag. I wanted to show how language shapes whose experiences are recognized and who feels represented.

</TextBlock>

## Result

These graphics give the advocacy side of my practice a public voice alongside client work. They’re also starting points for longer conversations and articles.
