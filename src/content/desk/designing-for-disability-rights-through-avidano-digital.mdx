Through my independent practice, Avidano Digital, I’ve been creating social graphics about disability rights and inclusion. I wanted to challenge harmful accessibility narratives and make complex inclusive-design issues visible and memorable.

This gives me a way to share the thinking behind my work beyond a client project. The graphics make that perspective public, with room to be direct about things I think we need to question.

## Who are we making the case for?

One of the graphics challenges a familiar argument: accessibility helps everyone. That can be true, but disabled people should not have to prove that everyone else benefits before their access matters.

The message is simple: “Accessibility is a human right, not a bonus feature.” I want the conversation to begin with the people who face barriers and their right to participate.

Another graphic uses the disability-rights message “Nothing about us without us.” It puts participation at the center of inclusion. Disabled people should have a say in the decisions that affect them.



## Giving the message a visual voice

I used bold color and large type to give the main message room. Some graphics use a short statement; others include more explanation. The visual language changes with the subject while the Avidano Digital branding connects the work.

The “Accessibility isn’t a game” graphic uses pixel-art characters to question the idea that accessibility is a score to chase. “Inclusion is not one-dimensional” uses the Progress Pride flag to bring overlapping identities into the conversation.

These are small pieces of communication, so the wording carries a lot of responsibility. A short statement needs to make its point without reducing the people or issues behind it to a slogan.



## Result

I now have a set of social graphics that makes the advocacy side of Avidano Digital visible alongside my client work. They give me a starting point for longer conversations and articles about the accessibility claims and assumptions I want to challenge.
