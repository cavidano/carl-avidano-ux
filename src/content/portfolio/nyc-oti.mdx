## NYC Office of Technology and Innovation

As a Senior UX Designer with the New York City Office of Technology and Innovation (OTI), I collaborated across teams and city agencies. I designed and delivered digital services used by New Yorkers and the city staff who support them.

My work included internal tools used by city agencies and public-facing websites. Each project had different goals, but all focused on making complex city services easier to use.

</CaseStudyOverview>

<TextBlock>

## Materials for the Arts

Materials for the Arts (MFTA), located in Queens, is NYC's primary creative reuse center. MFTA supports arts and education across the five boroughs. It gives donated materials to arts nonprofits, public schools, and city agencies.

</TextBlock>

<TextBlock>

### Public website and donation management portal

When I joined the project, development had been stalled for nearly two years. I collaborated with the project manager and MFTA staff in Queens. We worked together to document workflows. We gathered requirements and found issues. We also identified opportunities to improve the manual donation process.

I led UX strategy, design, and front-end development for a new city-branded website. I also created a custom donation management portal. This made the experience smoother for donors, participants, staff, and warehouse volunteers.

</TextBlock>

## Notify NYC

Notify NYC is New York City's official program for emergency communications. It is managed by New York City Emergency Management (NYCEM). The service gives residents and visitors important alerts, service updates, and emergency information.

### Public-facing website and admin portal

I led UX design and front-end development for the public website and the admin platform used by NYCEM staff. I improved the enrollment process for residents. I also designed tools for staff to manage and publish emergency alerts.

</TextBlock>

## NYC DoRIS

The NYC Department of Records and Information Services (DoRIS) preserves the city's historical and government records. Its collection is an important resource for residents, researchers, historians, and the public.

</TextBlock>

<TextBlock>

### Order vital records

I led UX design for the Order Vital Records modernization. This online service lets residents, researchers, and historians request historical records and archival materials. I worked with stakeholders and teamed up with a React developer to develop the new interface. Together, we turned a complicated government process into a guided digital experience.

The project focused on forms that adapted to user needs. The forms helped people select record types, understand eligibility requirements, provide supporting documents, and complete checkout.

</TextBlock>

## NYC PoleTop Manager

PoleTop Manager was a GIS platform for reserving space on city-owned poles for telecom network equipment.

The system helped city staff and telecom partners manage city-owned utility poles across the five boroughs. It supported their work on pole-space reservations, construction, and telecom deployments.

I led UX strategy and front-end development. I worked closely with OTI stakeholders to turn specialized infrastructure workflows into a user-friendly GIS platform. My work included dashboards, maps, asset views, and workflows for managing thousands of city-owned poles.

</TextBlock>

## Looking back

My time with the City shaped my passion for accessibility and inclusive design. It challenged me to think beyond individual screens and features and consider how digital services can better serve people from all backgrounds, abilities, and circumstances.

Many of the ideas that would eventually become the Natura11y design system took root during this time, particularly the importance of building accessibility into the foundation rather than treating it as an afterthought.

I'm grateful for the opportunity to work alongside city agencies and public servants in the greatest city in the world.

</TextBlock>
