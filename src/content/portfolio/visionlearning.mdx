## Challenge

Visionlearning provides free bilingual STEM learning resources for students and educators worldwide. Its outdated, non-responsive website had become increasingly difficult to maintain and constrained the organization’s ability to introduce new educational content and learning experiences. While they had a large body of science content, it was hard to understand what was available, who it was for, and where to start.

## Solution

Over a multi-year engagement, I led UX design and front-end development to help students and educators find and use Visionlearning’s science resources in English and Spanish. The work began with its visual identity and continued through navigation and reading layouts, interactive tools, scientific illustration, and support for module authors.

## Results

The platform supports English and Spanish learning resources through responsive reading layouts, glossary and NGSS highlighting, interactive tools, and scientific illustrations. The 2016 follow-up audit documented improvements in course organization, registration visibility, and interface consistency. Continued design work addressed the needs of readers, teachers, and module authors across later releases.

</CaseStudyOverview>

## Logo and brand guidelines

The engagement began with the logo redesign. I created a mark to suggest both a student engrossed in learning and an observant eye. It gave Visionlearning a recognizable identity that I carried through the website's typography, color, and page layouts.

We also developed comprehensive brand guidelines covering logo use, typography, color, imagery, and scientific illustration. The guidelines connected that identity to the website’s navigation, reading layouts, and quiz states, giving the team shared guidance as the platform evolved.

## Mapping the website and its content

We organized Visionlearning around Library, Glossary, and Classroom, giving each area a distinct purpose. The Library brought together learning modules, interactive animations, and research videos. Modules could be searched or browsed by discipline, while the Glossary provided a direct route to scientific definitions.

The map also considers how that content would be managed. A module brings together reading, figures, quiz questions, and supporting resources. Glossary entries and shared media have their own editing paths because they can appear in more than one module.

I worked directly with the backend developer on these relationships. The annotations record decisions about publishing and maintaining content, alongside questions we still needed to resolve.

</TextBlock>

## Designing the authoring workflow

Those learning experiences also needed a practical way to create and maintain the content. I designed administration wireframes for organizing disciplines and modules, then editing each module through Module Info, Reading, Quiz, and Resources.

The reading editor accommodates structured sections, figures, English and Spanish audio, references, and further reading. Resource groups can be added and rearranged as a module develops.

The quiz editor connects directly to the feedback shown to readers. Authors can mark the correct answer and write a separate explanation for each answer choice. Keeping the explanations with the question makes them part of authoring the quiz.

## Refining the designs through feedback

The work continued through multiple redesigns over more than a decade. I presented and refined layouts as the organization’s needs evolved, using research findings to guide subsequent changes.

Kim Krause Berg of Creative Vision Web Consulting conducted usability research and audits. The 2015 testing found that people could struggle to understand what Visionlearning offered, where to begin, and how to use Classroom. Participants asked for clearer lesson organization and instructions; registration and mobile use also presented barriers.

The research combined:

- Task-based usability testing and participant comments.
- Expert review of navigation, content, mobile behavior, and accessibility.
- A follow-up audit checking the redesign against the earlier recommendations.

We added a clear purpose statement to the homepage, refined course organization, and made registration more visible. The 2016 follow-up confirmed the purpose statement and documented improvements in course organization, registration visibility, and interface consistency. Getting-started guidance, accessibility, and performance still needed work.

</TextBlock>

## Building tools around the reading

The client requested glossary highlighting and annotations connecting the content to the Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS). I designed the interfaces and wrote the front-end behavior for these different reading and teaching tasks.

With glossary highlighting enabled, readers can select a term and see its definition without leaving the module. The NGSS mode highlights passages connected to science standards and displays the relevant information for educators. Only one highlighting mode is active at a time, keeping the two kinds of annotation distinct.

The annotated Earth Structure source connects passages to practices such as analyzing data and constructing explanations. The NGSS interface makes that teaching context available beside the reading.

### Exploring the periodic table

I also designed and built an interactive periodic table. Readers can select an element to open its details while keeping the table available as a reference. The table view preserves the relationships between elements; Search and List provides another way to find and filter them.

The demonstration below shows keyboard navigation, Carbon's details, and a search filtered to noble gases.

## Explaining science through imagery

I created scientific illustrations in Adobe Illustrator and Photoshop, ranging from small supporting diagrams to detailed cell anatomy, molecular structures, and experimental setups. The figures needed to work within the reading layouts as well as explain the science.

Plant and animal cell illustrations use labels and a consistent visual treatment to identify structures. Other figures show relationships or sequences, such as the stages of an experiment or the differences between RNA and DNA.

Some visuals were entirely custom. Others combined carefully selected, high-resolution imagery with labels and scientific concepts. I adapted the approach to what each module needed to explain; the collection below represents part of a much larger body of work across the site.

### Adapting graphics to the lesson

The periodic-table lessons required a connected set of graphics. I adapted the table to emphasize element families, electron blocks, and patterns such as atomic radius, with supporting diagrams explaining electron structure and notation.

Each view directs attention to a different relationship: a highlighted family remains visible within the full table, while changes in circle size make relative atomic size easier to compare. The series includes English and Spanish versions.

## Carrying the work into Natura11y

In later redesigns, I built the public-site front end on Natura11y, the design system I created. Shared components supported the page layouts and interface behavior, while Visionlearning’s reading tools and scientific content addressed the needs of this particular platform.

The backend developer subsequently adopted Natura11y for the teacher-facing CMS interface. That extended the same design system into another part of the platform, maintained by another developer.

Visionlearning is one of the client projects where I put Natura11y into practice. The next case study explains how that work sits within the broader design ecosystem, including its components, design libraries, and documentation.

## Looking back

The long-term partnership let me see how the work was used and revisit it through later redesigns. As the organization's needs grew, I could refine the reading experience, respond to specific teaching needs, and build tools to support new uses of the content.


Projects like Visionlearning are rare. Few opportunities allow me to combine so many of my interests in a single project: design, user experience, front-end development, and digital illustration.

This project gave me a deep appreciation for science education, Next Generation Science Standards, and the challenge of making complex topics easier to understand. I'm grateful for the opportunity to contribute to a resource that helps students, educators, and lifelong learners better understand science.
