## Challenge

Visionlearning is a free, bilingual STEM learning platform for teachers and students worldwide. When they approached me in 2012, their website felt outdated and did not work on mobile devices. The site had become difficult to maintain, limiting their ability to introduce new educational content and learning experiences.

While they had a large body of valuable educational material, it was hard to understand what was available and who it was for.

## Solution

Through Avidano Digital, I worked with Visionlearning to create a new visual identity that reflects their mission.

We organized the website architecture and content management workflow around three distinct areas: Library, Glossary, and Classroom. We redesigned the site with a mobile-first approach, paying particular attention to how the layouts accommodated Spanish content.

This multi-year engagement included regular releases and enhancements to the platform. For the latest iteration, we moved the front end onto Natura11y, an accessible design system, to build custom learning experiences and interactions aligned with the Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS).

I also created scientific illustrations, improved support for mathematical equations, and worked with the developer to design the module authoring experience.

## Results

The new website supports learning in English and Spanish on desktop and mobile. Visionlearning also has a more efficient workflow for creating and updating learning modules. A follow-up audit documented clearer course organization, more visible registration, and a more consistent interface.

## Refining the designs through feedback

Kim Krause Berg of Creative Vision Web Consulting conducted usability research and audits. The findings highlighted confusion about the site’s purpose, lesson organization, and Classroom, plus barriers to registration and mobile use.

The research combined:

- Task-based usability testing and participant comments.
- Expert review of navigation, content, mobile behavior, and accessibility.
- A follow-up audit checking the redesign against the earlier recommendations.

</TextBlock>

## Building tools around the reading

The client requested glossary highlighting and NGSS annotations within the learning modules. I designed the interfaces and wrote the front-end behavior.

Readers can turn on glossary highlighting to open definitions beside the lesson. Educators can use NGSS mode to see the standards associated with a passage. Only one mode is active at a time, keeping the two kinds of highlighting distinct.

### Adapting graphics to the lesson

The periodic-table lessons required a connected set of graphics. I adapted the table to emphasize element families, electron blocks, and patterns such as atomic radius, with supporting diagrams explaining electron structure and notation.

Each view directs attention to a different relationship: a highlighted family remains visible within the full table, while changes in circle size make relative atomic size easier to compare. The series includes English and Spanish versions.

</TextBlock>

## Looking back

The long-term partnership let me see how the work was used and revisit it through later redesigns. As the organization's needs grew, I could refine the reading experience, respond to specific teaching needs, and build tools to support new uses of the content.

Projects like Visionlearning are rare. Few opportunities allow me to combine so many of my interests in a single project: design, user experience, front-end development, and digital illustration.

This project gave me a deep appreciation for science education, Next Generation Science Standards, and the challenge of making complex topics easier to understand. I'm grateful for the opportunity to contribute to a resource that helps students, educators, and lifelong learners better understand science.

</TextBlock>
