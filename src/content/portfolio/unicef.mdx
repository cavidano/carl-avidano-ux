## Challenge

UNICEF’s National Committees needed fundraising materials they could share with donors. Before the Funding Marketplace, gathering those materials was a manual process. It involved collecting program information, budgets, financial reports, and stories from country offices. UNICEF needed a shared way to prepare and maintain the content, and a way to help fundraising teams find what they needed.

## Solution

I worked with UNICEF staff, project leadership, and Quark developers to create the Funding Marketplace. We created a central hub for finding and comparing donor toolkits. Next, we added a dashboard to help offices create, review, and publish content.

I was the sole UX and front-end designer for the project. I created the information architecture, workflows, wireframes, and interfaces. I also built the front-end templates and worked with the development team to review their implementation.

## Results
The Funding Marketplace provided National Committees and Country Offices with a central hub for donor materials. They could browse donor toolkits, compare programme information, and manage content collaboratively. The dashboard made assignments, document status, and review actions visible within the workflow. Later releases refined these interactions as the system developed.

## Phase 2: Country Office Dashboard

As the platform evolved, UNICEF needed administrative tools. These tools would help Country Offices and National Committees manage content, reporting, approvals, and collaboration workflows.

</TextBlock>

<TextBlock>

### Dublin workshop

I joined a multi-day workshop in Dublin. There, I collaborated with UNICEF stakeholders, project team members, and Quark developers. Together, we mapped the screens and responsibilities for the Country Office Dashboard. We worked through how content would move between offices and which actions each role needed to create, review, and publish it.

</TextBlock>

<FigureSingle caption="Whiteboards from the Dublin workshop.">

<TextBlock>

### Wireframes

I used wireframes to work through assignments, toolkit documents, and supporting materials. Each content type needed its own controls while remaining part of a consistent dashboard.

We removed extra subpages during the refinements. I also added available actions to the selected table row. The options depended on the user's role, the document's state, and whether editing, comments, or approval had been requested.

</TextBlock>

<FigureSingle caption="Wireframes and user flow documentation.">

![Dashboard wireframes showing assignments, toolkit management, supporting materials, and comment requests.](../../images/unicef/unicef-wireframe-flows.png)

</FigureSingle>

<TextBlock>

### Reviewing content across offices

The comment workflow needed to show who had been asked to review a document and whose response was still pending. I designed the workflow for selecting reviewers, tracking their feedback, and adding their comments to the document.

The wireframes also covered less straightforward situations. If the sender added returned comments to the document before all reviewers replied, the remaining reviewers’ assignments showed a warning. Confirmation messages appeared in the selected row. This kept feedback near the document being managed.

</TextBlock>

### Working with developers

I provided HTML, CSS, and JavaScript templates. I was responsible for front-end quality assurance and worked with Quark developers to implement the front end. I compared the screens we implemented with the templates. I noted the differences in layout and behavior.

For example, a dropdown's longer lists extended beyond the visible page. I compared the live behavior to the provided template. This gave developers a clear reference for how the control should work.

</TextBlock>
