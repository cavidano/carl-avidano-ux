## Challenge

The Legal Aid Disaster Resource Center (LADRC) is part of the Legal Services Corporation. LADRC needed to boost engagement and make disaster recovery legal resources easier to find. This would help people seeking assistance during stressful times.

## Solution

As UX Director at Reingold, I planned and conducted remote user interviews, usability testing, and tree testing. This helped us understand where users struggled with the existing navigation. I translated our findings into a recommendations presentation for the client. I created low-fidelity wireframes focused on clearer search, navigation, and resource access.

## Results

The findings gave LADRC clear priorities for improving access to legal resources. We made search easier to find on mobile. We clarified navigation labels and improved usability of resource cards across the site.

</CaseStudyOverview>

## Research and discovery

This was my first project at Reingold, and discovery was already underway. I worked with the team to understand how people found legal help and where the existing site made that difficult.

- **Remote interviews and usability testing:** I organized and led Zoom sessions with disaster survivors. I paired interviews with usability tasks on the current site.
- **In-person usability testing:** We conducted four days of testing at an LSC conference in Des Moines, Iowa.
- **Tree testing:** We used <LinkOpenNew LinkUrl='https://www.optimalworkshop.com' LinkText='Optimal Workshop' /> to see if people could find resources using the navigation labels and groupings. This way, the page design wouldn’t affect their choices.

### Findings and recommendations

I worked with the UX and analytics teams to develop the recommendations. We combined what people said and did in testing with analytics showing how visitors used the site. The content and technical leads contributed an audit of 100 resources, keyword research, and an evaluation of search.

Together, these findings shaped recommendations for navigation, content, and resource access. I created a presentation of the findings and recommendations. I also turned our suggestions into low-fidelity wireframes.

### Low-fidelity wireframes

Usability and tree testing revealed confusion about navigation categories. In the wireframes, I made the labels clearer. This helped show who each section was for. I also grouped related categories together. New landing pages described the resources in each section. The homepage highlighted Recent Disasters and made it easier for survivors and legal advocates to find help.

</TextBlock>

### Making search visible on mobile

Remote testing showed that disaster survivors struggled to find the search option on mobile. It was hidden in the menu. I separated Search and Menu into distinct controls. The proposed interaction allowed people to search directly. They didn’t need to open or explore the navigation first.

### Making disaster information easier to use

Analytics showed that many visitors came directly to the Recent Disasters pages. They didn’t explore other sections. I organized the content on these pages around locations, deadlines, and available help. I kept the “On This Page” links that legal professionals found useful.

</TextBlock>

## Looking back

The research we conducted with LADRC reinforced an often-overlooked UX principle: listen first.

We conducted research with users in various settings, both in person and online. This helped us find barriers, confirm recommendations, and discover opportunities we might otherwise have missed. The experience showed that user feedback can be consistent across different research methods when you ask the right questions.

I’m thankful that Reingold values research based on evidence. I appreciate the chance to work on projects that put real users at the center. It was especially rewarding to help shape a resource based directly on the needs and experiences of the people it served.
