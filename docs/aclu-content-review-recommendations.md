# ACLU tailored portfolio page: content review and proposed rewrite

Reviewed: August 9, 2026

Scope: editorial recommendations for the private `/aclu` page. This document does not recommend a redesign or changes to Natura11y markup or behavior.

## Executive assessment

The page already makes a credible case for Carl. Its strongest idea is that accessibility, research, design systems, and implementation are not separate specialties in his practice. The four selected projects substantiate that idea with public-service work, and the opening connects accessibility to civil rights without treating disability as adversity. Natura11y is especially relevant because it demonstrates long-term, hands-on systems thinking across Figma, reusable components, documentation, accessibility, and production code.

The largest copy problem is repetition without enough added evidence. The page repeatedly lists “research, systems thinking, accessibility, stakeholder alignment, and implementation,” then repeats those ideas in nine accordion panels. Several passages sound like résumé summaries rather than Carl speaking to a specific product team. The copy also blurs individual and team contributions in a few places—most notably the Phoenix accessibility result and the claim that Carl “aligned” more than 40 departments. Those issues can make otherwise strong evidence feel less precise.

The revision below makes the hiring argument more direct:

- Carl turns research into product direction.
- He has designed and shipped consequential public-service products.
- He can connect Figma, accessible patterns, documentation, and production code.
- He works closely with engineers and can evaluate implementation quality.
- He can explain decisions and constraints to many kinds of stakeholders.
- His accessibility practice is grounded in civil rights, product quality, professional expertise, and lived experience.
- He has current management and mentorship experience in addition to hands-on craft.

## Essential revisions

1. **Clarify Carl’s role on every project card.** Replace category lists with short statements that distinguish what Carl led, what he contributed, and what the team achieved.
2. **Correct the Phoenix attribution.** Carl led UX strategy and conducted interviews across 40+ departments; he did not independently “align” all of those departments or single-handedly produce the launch result. Attribute WCAG results to the redesigned site and team.
3. **Use WCAG terminology carefully.** Prefer “conformance” to “compliance.” Verify the scope and method behind “100% AA and 97% AAA” before retaining the percentages.
4. **Make the hero about contribution, not a general statement of values.** Keep “disabled designer,” but connect it to Carl’s accessibility practice rather than using disability to support a broad claim about what rights mean.
5. **Replace abstract capability lists with evidence.** The current relevant-work introduction and why-hire introduction use nearly the same collection of nouns. State the through-line once; let project and capability copy prove it.
6. **Streamline the accordions.** Use seven core panels instead of nine. Remove “Data visualization,” “Digital illustration,” and “Mission-driven work” as standalone panels. The first is a supporting communication skill, the advocacy figure already demonstrates the second, and the entire page should demonstrate the third. Add “Leadership and mentorship,” which the résumé supports and the role values.
7. **Surface end-to-end and engineering evidence without overclaiming.** Carl has direct evidence of leading UX design and front-end work on selected NYC OTI products. Avoid implying that he independently owned every phase or every product named.
8. **Keep AI out of the page’s core argument and review the linked application path.** Natura11y, the selected work, and Carl’s research and accessibility practices stand on their own and predate generative AI. The tailored page does not foreground AI, but its About and Natura11y links currently do. Decide whether that emphasis helps this specific application. Separately consider whether listing Claude, Cursor, and Codex on the submitted résumé distracts from a posting that explicitly expects unaided proficiency.

## Optional refinements

- Change “Why should the ACLU hire me?” to “What I would bring to the ACLU.” It is confident, specific to contribution, and less like a generic application prompt.
- Use sentence case for “About me” in the button and hidden heading for consistency.
- Add “Based in New York” to the biography if local availability is useful to confirm for this hybrid role. It is supported by the résumé but should not crowd the hero.
- Improve the advocacy image alternative text with representative messages from the image. The caption should explain why Carl made the work; the alternative text should identify what the collage contains.
- Change the footer to “All rights reserved.” only as a sitewide copy-style refinement. It has no bearing on this application.

## Recommended cuts and consolidation

1. **Cut “Data visualization” as a standalone accordion.** Carl has relevant visual-communication evidence—research decks, system diagrams, dashboards, maps, and GIS interfaces—but the phrase can suggest a statistical visualization specialty that the evidence does not establish. The strongest examples can remain in their case studies.
2. **Cut “Digital illustration” as a standalone accordion.** The advocacy figure already demonstrates this skill more memorably, and the ACLU role is hiring primarily for product design. Visionlearning and the Natura11y icon library remain available in the full portfolio.
3. **Cut “Mission-driven work” as a standalone accordion.** The hero, selected projects, biography, and advocacy figure should make this visible. Repeating the sector list at the end weakens rather than strengthens the case.
4. **Mention Carl’s disability identity once in primary copy.** Keep it in the hero. Use the accessibility panel to show the work rather than restating identity.
5. **Do not repeat the same five-part capability list.** The relevant-work introduction should explain the project through-line; the closing introduction should explain how Carl works; the accordion should supply evidence.
6. **Avoid repeating every project name in multiple panels.** Phoenix is the clearest evidence for research and stakeholder communication; NYC OTI is the clearest evidence for product delivery and engineering; Natura11y is the clearest evidence for systems. Use each where it proves a distinct point.

If Carl prefers to retain all current capability topics, use these restrained fallback versions rather than the current claims:

**Visual communication**

> I use findings decks, system diagrams, dashboards, maps, and interface examples to help teams understand research and complex product relationships. My LADRC, Natura11y, and NYC OTI work shows this range without positioning me as a specialist in statistical data visualization.

**Illustration and icon design**

> My visual-design background includes scientific illustrations for Visionlearning, Natura11y’s icon library, and disability-rights advocacy graphics. These skills help me explain complex ideas and create clear, consistent product visuals.

**Public-interest experience**

> My work spans disaster legal aid, UNICEF, city and state government, public education, healthcare, and nonprofit advocacy. I understand that products involving rights, safety, and essential services require careful research, accessible design, clear communication, and accountability to the people who use them.

## How the revision responds to the ACLU role

The revised page aligns with the role through evidence rather than by mirroring the posting. The Phoenix and LADRC copy shows research becoming information architecture, recommendations, and wireframes. NYC OTI demonstrates end-to-end product work and direct engineering collaboration on consequential services. Natura11y shows that Carl can work within and evolve a design system across Figma, accessibility patterns, components, documentation, and production technologies. The accessibility panel treats WCAG as part of design and acceptance quality, while the stakeholder and leadership panels show that Carl can communicate across disciplines, invite feedback, and mentor other designers.

The mission fit is present throughout the project selection: city services, emergency communications, disaster legal aid, education, healthcare, and public-interest systems. The page does not need to claim that Carl alone can solve civil-rights access problems. It needs to show that he can bring a mature, collaborative, accessible product-design practice to the ACLU’s national and affiliate websites. The proposed copy makes that case clearly and credibly.
