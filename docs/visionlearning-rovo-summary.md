## Timeline correction

Carl clarified that Visionlearning was redesigned multiple times over more than a decade to support the organization's growing needs. The engagement lasted more than ten years; a single redesign did not take ten years. The transcript's opening wording is misleading and must not be reused in the case study.

## Editorial decisions

- Describe how Library, Glossary, and Classroom gave the content distinct purposes and made the audience clearer.
- Connect the learning tools to what readers and educators can do, while preserving the specific details supported by Carl's account and the repository review.
- Keep the CMS developer's adoption of Natura11y as a qualitative outcome. Do not infer measured learning gains or accessibility conformance.
- Use “My role” for a plain-language paragraph about Carl's contribution and the organization through which he did the work. Keep project context in the Challenge.
- Preserve the existing figures, their order, and intentional background colors.

The local draft incorporates these decisions. The Confluence review copy incorporates the same edits; the source transcript below is preserved separately from the edited case study.

## Original supplied transcript

The following retains the transcription errors as supplied. The feedback question at the end came from the transcript interface.

> This case study is about a decade long redesign of vision learning, a bilingual science education platform, to make its content easier to find, use, and teach from.
>
> The core problem was structural and practical.
>
> Vision learning had a large body of science content, but the site made it hard to understand what was available, who it was for and where to start.
>
> It was also not responsive, so his mobile East group, the experience lacked.
>
> The redesign addressed that by organizing the site into 3 clear areas, library, glossary, and classroom.
>
> And by using plain language to signal that the platform serve both students and educators.
>
> Within the library, content is further grouped into learning modules, interactive animations and research videos.
>
> Then organized by disciplines such as biology, chemistry, earth science, and physics.
>
> That matters because it turned a collection of links into a navigable learning system.
>
> The work went well beyond information architecture.
>
> It included a logo redesign and a broader visual identity, responsive templates for English and Spanish, and front end patterns that kept long readings, figures, quizzes, and reference tools usable across screen sizes.
>
> On desktop, reading tools sat beside the text, on mobile, they moved into collapsible tasks, so they stay accessible without taking over the screen.
>
> The most distinctive contribution was the learning tools built into the reading experience.
>
> Readers could open glossary definitions without leading the module.
>
> Educators could do annotations, ties to next generation science standards, and an interactive periodic table, let users explore elements through both a table view and a searchable list.
>
> Mathjack support made equations readable and better exposed to assistive technology, and custom scientific illustrations helped explain complex concepts visually.
>
> The broader result is a responsive bilingual platform, with clearer pathways into science content, and reusable front-end patterns.
>
> Those patterns were strong enough that the backend CMS developer also adopted natural 11 Y. For the teacher facing interface.
>
> Bottom line, this was not just a visual refresh.
>
> It was a full redesign structure, tools, and shared development patterns that made vision learning more coherent, teachable, and scalable.
